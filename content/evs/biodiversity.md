# Biodiversity

Biodiversity is defined as, "the variety and variability among all groups of living organisms and the ecosystem in which they occur.

"Bio" means 'life' and "Diversity" means 'variety'. Hence, biodiversity refers to the wide variety of life on the earth.


**Status:** Our planet contains more than 20 million species, but only about 1.4 million species have been identified so far.

---

## CLASSIFICATION (OR) TYPES OF BIODIVERSITY

Biodiversity is generally classified into three types:

1)Genetic diversity.

2)Species diversity.

3)Community (or) Ecosystem diversity.

---

## 1. Genetic Diversity

Genetic diversity is the diversity within species, i.e., variation of genes within the species.

These differences are due to differences in the combination of genes.

**Examples:**

**I) Rice varieties:** All rice varieties belong to the species "Oryza sativa", but there are thousands of varieties differing in size, shape, and nutrient content.

**II) Teak wood varieties:** Indian teak, Burma teak, Malaysian teak.

---

## 2. Species Diversity

Species diversity is the diversity between different species.

The sum of varieties of all the living organisms at the species level is known as species diversity.

**Examples:**

**I) Plant species:** Apple, Mango, Grapes, Wheat, Rice.

**II) Animal species:** Lion, Tiger, Elephant, Deer.

---

## 3. Community (or) Ecosystem Diversity

The diversity at the ecological (or) habitat level is known as ecosystem diversity.

It explains the interaction between living organisms and the physical environment.

**Example:** River ecosystem (includes fish, aquatic insects, and variety of plants).

---

## VALUES OF BIODIVERSITY

The values of biodiversity are classified as follows:

**1. Consumptive Use Value**

These are direct use values, where the biodiversity products are harvested and consumed directly.

**Food:** Nearly 80-90% of our food crops have been domesticated from tropical wild plants.

**Drugs:** Around 70% of modern medicines are derived from plant extracts.

**Fuel:** Firewoods are directly consumed by villagers and tribals.

**2. Productive Use Value**

Biodiversity products that have obtained a commercial value and are marketed/sold.

**3. Social Values**

These values are associated with the social life, religion, and spiritual aspects of the people.

**Holy Plants:** Tulsi, Peepal, Lotus, Bael.

**Holy Animals:** Cow, Snake, Bull, Peacock.

**4. Ethical (or) Existence Value**

It involves ethical issues like "all life must be preserved".

Even if we are not deriving anything directly from Kangaroo, Zebra, or Giraffe, we feel that these should exist in nature.

**5. Aesthetic Value**

The beautiful nature of plants and animals insists us to protect biodiversity.

**Eco-tourism:** People spend time and money to visit beautiful areas (e.g., to see colors of butterflies, peacocks).
