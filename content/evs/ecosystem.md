# Ecosystem

An **ecosystem** is a community of different species interacting with one another and with their physical (abiotic) environment, exchanging energy and matter in a systematic manner.


![This is an alt text.](https://www.poriyaan.in/media/imgPori/images13/nBgSvcm.png "This is a sample image.")


## Types of Ecosystem

**1. Natural Ecosystem**

Natural ecosystems operate under natural environmental conditions without human control.

**a) Terrestrial ecosystem**

These ecosystems occur on land and are mainly influenced by climate and vegetation.

**Examples:** Forest, grassland, desert.

**b) Aquatic ecosystem**
These ecosystems occur in water and are classified based on salt content.

There are 2 types of Aquatic ecosystem. They are:

**I) Freshwater ecosystem**

**Running water:** Rivers and streams

**Standing water:** Ponds and lakes

**II) Marine ecosystem**

Seas and oceans containing salt water.


**2. Artificial (Man-made) Ecosystem**

Artificial ecosystems are created and maintained by humans to meet their needs.

**Examples:** Croplands, gardens, aquariums.

## Structure of an Ecosystem

The structure of an ecosystem refers to the organization of biotic and abiotic components and their relationships.

**A. Abiotic Components**

These are the non-living physical and chemical factors that influence organisms.

**I)Physical factors** include air, water, soil, sunlight and temperature which support growth and survival.

**II)Chemical factors** include organic substances (proteins, carbohydrates, lipids) and inorganic elements such as C, H, O, N, P, K which are essential for life processes.

![This is an alt text.](https://picture-search.skywork.ai/aippt/image/sheet/19ec0952-6b11-4cb6-aaff-fd515498a39a.png "This is a sample image.")

**B. Biotic Components**

These are the living organisms present in an ecosystem and are classified based on their mode of nutrition.

**1. Producers (Autotrophs)**

Producers prepare their own food by photosynthesis using sunlight and chlorophyll and form the base of the food chain.

**Examples:** Green plants, algae.

**Photosynthesis**

The green pigments called chlorophyll, present in the leaves of plants, converts CO2 and H2 O in the presence of sunlight into carbohydrates.

![This is an alt text.](https://www.easysevens.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/Photosynthesis-Diagram.jpg?swcfpc=1 "This is a sample image.")

**2. Consumers (Heterotrophs)**

Consumers cannot synthesize their own food and depend directly or indirectly on producers.


**I) Primary consumers (herbivores):** Goat, deer, cow


**II) Secondary consumers (carnivores):** Frog, cat, snake


**III) Tertiary consumers (top carnivores):** Lion, tiger

**3. Decomposers**

Decomposers feed on dead plants and animals and break them into simpler substances. They return nutrients to the soil and water for reuse by producers.

**Examples:** Bacteria, fungi.

## Functions of an Ecosystem

**1. Primary Production**

Green plants convert solar energy into chemical energy by photosynthesis.

**2. Secondary Production**

Energy is transferred from producers to consumers through food chains.

**3. Decomposition and Nutrient Cycling**

Organic matter is decomposed and nutrients are recycled back into the ecosystem.

**4. Energy Flow**

Energy flows in one direction from sun -> producers -> consumers -> decomposers.
