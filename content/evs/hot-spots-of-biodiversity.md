# Hot Spots of Biodiversity

The hot spots are the geographic areas which possess high endemic species (species found only in that place).

These are the most remarkable and threatened areas.

Many of them have been reduced to less than 10% of their original vegetation.

---

## Criteria for Recognising Hot Spots

To call an area a "Hot Spot", it must meet these rules:

I) Richness of endemic species (Primary criterion).

II) The site must be under threat.

III) It should contain important gene pools of potentially useful plants.

---

## Global Status

These hot spots cover less than 2% of the world's land but contain 50,000 endemic species.

Myers et al. (2000) defined that an area must contain at least 0.5% of endemic plant species to be a hotspot.

## HOT SPOTS OF BIODIVERSITY IN INDIA

India has two major biodiversity hot spots:

1. Eastern Himalayas (Indo-Burma region).
2. Western Ghats (Sri Lanka region).

---

## Eastern Himalayas

**Location:** Covers Nepal, Bhutan, and Northern India.

**Plant Diversity:** There are 35,000 plant species found here, of which 30% are endemic.

**Key Features:**

This region is rich in wild plants of economic value.

**Origin of Crops:** Rice, banana, citrus, ginger, chilli, jute, and sugarcane.

**Medicinal Value:** The Taxol yielding plant (used for cancer cure) is found here.

**Animal Diversity:**

~ 63% of mammals are from Eastern Himalayas.

~ 60% of Indian birds are from North East.

---

## Western Ghats

**Location:** Covers Maharashtra, Karnataka, Tamil Nadu, and Kerala.

**Plant Diversity:** Nearly 1,500 endemic dicotyledon plant species are found here.

**Animal Diversity:**

~ 62% amphibians (frogs, etc.) and 50% lizards are endemic here.

**Threat:** Only 6.8% of the original forests exist today, the rest has been deforested or degraded.

**Examples:**

**1.Plants:** Ternstroemia japonica, Rhododendron.

**2.Animals:** Blue bird, Lizard, Hawk.
